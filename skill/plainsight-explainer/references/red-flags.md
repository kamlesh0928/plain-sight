# Common red flags in Indian digital-payment scams

Use these as patterns to look for. One strong flag is enough for `likely_scam`. Several weak flags together should be at least `careful`.

## Strong flags

- **Asks for OTP, UPI PIN, card PIN, CVV or password.** Real banks and payment apps never ask for these by SMS, link or phone call.
- **"Enter your UPI PIN to receive money."** Receiving money never needs a PIN. This is a collect request that takes money from the user.
- **Collect request from an unknown person** that looks like a payment received or a refund.
- **Fee before a benefit:** processing fee, insurance, tax or "registration" to get a loan, prize, lottery or job.
- **Asks to install a remote-access app** (for example screen-sharing apps) or an APK file sent by message.
- **"Wrong transfer, please send it back"** from an unknown number, especially with a UPI ID to return the money to.
- **Threat plus link:** account will be blocked, power will be cut tonight, SIM will be disabled, legal action will start, unless the user taps a link or calls a number now.

## Medium flags

- Shortened or look-alike links (misspelled brand names, extra words in the domain).
- Sender is a personal mobile number but claims to be a bank, utility or government office.
- "Customer care" number inside the message instead of the number printed on the card or in the official app.
- Pressure to act within minutes or "today".
- Offers that are too good to be true (large cashback, guaranteed returns, free gifts).
- Spelling and grammar mistakes in a message that claims to be official.
- Asks to scan a QR code to receive money.

## Usually fine, but still advise care

- Debit or credit alerts that show a masked account number and say "if not you, call the number on your card".
- Recharge, order and delivery confirmations that do not ask for anything.
- Bills that show a due date and tell the user to pay only through the official app or branch.
- A delivery OTP that is meant to be shared only with the delivery person at the door.

## Safe next steps to suggest

- Do not click links or call numbers in the message.
- Contact the bank or company using the number on the card, the bill or the official app.
- Never share OTP, PIN or password with anyone, including people who say they are from the bank.
- Report fraud at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930. Tell the bank quickly if money was lost.
